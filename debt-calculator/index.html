How does the debt payoff calculator work?

What it calculates

Each month, interest is added to your balance and your payment is subtracted. The calculator repeats this until the balance reaches zero, then reports the number of months, your debt-free date and the total interest you paid.

Why a small extra payment matters

Extra money goes straight to principal, so you pay interest on less each month. Even $50 can move your debt-free date closer.

Multiple debts?

This calculator treats your debts as one balance. For separate balances and snowball or avalanche strategies, use the debt payoff tracker and Debt Simulator in Paper & Prosperity.

How to use this calculator

Total debt amount: Add up all the balances you want to pay off — credit cards, personal loans, medical bills, or any fixed debt. Leave out your mortgage if you're focusing on consumer debt first.

Monthly payment: Enter what you're actually paying each month, not just the minimums. If you pay minimums only, the calculator will show you exactly why that's expensive — usually several years and thousands of dollars more in interest.

Average interest rate: If you have multiple debts, use a rough average. For example, one card at 24% and one at 18% averages to 21%. The calculator assumes a fixed rate, so it's an estimate — your actual payoff date will vary slightly as you pay down individual balances.

What do the results mean?

Months to payoff is the number of months until the balance hits zero, assuming no new charges and a consistent payment each month.

Debt-free date turns the month count into a calendar date so you can picture it — not just "43 months" but a specific month and year.

Total interest paid is how much extra you pay on top of the principal. This number surprises most people. A $10,000 balance at 22% APR with $250/month payments costs about $4,200 in interest before it's gone.

Add $50/month and save shows the interest saved and months cut if you increase your payment by $50. Even a small increase makes a significant difference early in the payoff.

Why manual calculators have limits

This calculator treats all your debt as one balance at one rate. That's useful for a quick estimate, but if you have three credit cards at different rates, the order you pay them off matters. The debt avalanche (highest interest first) saves the most money. The debt snowball (lowest balance first) builds momentum faster.

Paper & Prosperity's debt tracker lets you enter each balance separately, set your own payoff order, and see the exact date each debt disappears — plus a running total of interest saved. It's free and no account is required.

Common questions

Does this include minimum payment calculations?
No — enter the actual amount you plan to pay each month. If you only pay minimums, minimums change as your balance falls. This calculator assumes a fixed payment, so enter a fixed amount you can commit to.
What if my interest rate changes?
The calculator assumes a fixed APR. If you have a variable-rate card, use your current rate as an estimate. The result will be close enough to plan around, though the exact date will shift as rates change.
How is interest calculated?
Monthly: APR ÷ 12, applied to the remaining balance each period. This matches how most credit cards and personal loans calculate interest.

Debt payoff tips

Pick a strategy

Snowball pays the smallest balance first for quick wins. Avalanche pays the highest interest rate first and may reduce total interest.

How can I pay off debt faster?

Small changes add up. Paying even a little above the minimum each month cuts interest and moves your debt-free date closer. Redirecting a windfall, such as a tax refund or bonus, to the balance can save months.
