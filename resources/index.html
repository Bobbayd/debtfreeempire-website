Free Budgeting Resources

By Robert Daniels, Founder of Debt Free Empire · Updated October 2026

Debt Free Empire resources are free budgeting tools—spreadsheets, a debt payoff tracker, a savings tracker and more — everything to start a zero-based budget and reach debt free at your own pace.

Try Paper & Prosperity Free

How do I start with these resources?

If you're new to budgeting, start with the budget spreadsheet. It walks you through listing your income, planning your spending categories, and making sure your numbers add up to zero — every dollar assigned before the month starts.

If debt is the main problem, the debt payoff tracker is the better first stop. Enter each balance with its interest rate and minimum payment. The built-in simulator shows you your debt-free date and how extra payments change the timeline.

The savings goal tracker works alongside the budget. Create a separate vault for each goal — emergency fund, car repair, vacation — and update it monthly. Seeing named goals instead of one savings account makes it easier to protect the money.

Free vs. the app

The spreadsheets are standalone files — download once, use in Excel or Google Sheets, no account needed. They're good for people who prefer a spreadsheet or want to customize the layout.

Paper & Prosperity is the app version of the same system. It combines budget, debt tracker, and savings vaults in one place, runs on your phone and the web, and is also free. If you find yourself opening the spreadsheet every week, the app is worth trying — same concepts, faster to update on your phone.

Common questions

Are the spreadsheets really free?
The Etsy shop has both free and paid versions. The free listings are listed as free and require no payment. Some premium versions with extra features have a small price.
Do the spreadsheets work in Google Sheets?
Yes — the spreadsheets are built in Excel format and open in Google Sheets without issues. Formulas and formatting carry over correctly.
Is there a printable budget template?
Cash envelope printables with a monthly tracking sheet are coming soon. In the meantime, the budget spreadsheet can be printed from Excel or Google Sheets.

How do I build a simple budget in four steps?

1. Write down your monthly take-home income. 2. List your fixed bills, such as rent, utilities, insurance and minimum debt payments. 3. Add flexible spending like groceries, gas and fun money. 4. Subtract everything from your income and adjust until the result is zero, so every dollar has a job.

Review it once a week for a few minutes. A budget is not a punishment; it is a plan you can change. If you want it done with less typing, the Paper & Prosperity app is free and keeps your data on your device.
